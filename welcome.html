Pointer by 021

✓ Pointer is installed.

Show your coding agent exactly what to change. Point at your page, click Copy, and paste one picture with the source file:line of everything you marked into Claude Code, Cursor or any AI chat. Pointer stays off until you turn it on, on the tab you choose.

Start in four steps

  1. Open your app. Open the page you are building in Chrome. In development mode (npm run dev) your agent also gets the file and line.
  2. Turn Pointer on. Press Alt+P (on a Mac ⌃⇧P, Control+Shift+P), or click Pointer's icon. A dark bar appears at the bottom of the page: it is on.
  3. Show what you mean. Click where something should change, type what you want, press Enter. A numbered pin marks it. Add as many as you like.
  4. Copy and paste. Click Copy, then paste into your agent's chat: Ctrl+V in Claude Code's terminal (on a Mac too), ⌘V or Ctrl+V in Cursor and VS Code.

No icon in the toolbar? Click the puzzle piece next to the address bar and pin Pointer.

Pointer has no keyboard shortcut. Chrome gives an extension its key only when nothing else uses it: another extension, or one of Chrome's own keys. Choose one yourself: click the button, find Pointer, click the pencil and press the keys you want (for example Alt+P, on a Mac ⌃⇧P).

Is it working?

OnA dark bar sits at the bottom centre of the page, with Comment selected, and the mouse is a crosshair.
OffPress the shortcut again, click Pointer's icon again, or click the ✕ at the right end of the bar. Esc closes Pointer's own menus and goes back to the Page tool; there, Esc is the page's again.

Nothing happens?

A red ! on the iconChrome lets no extension run on this page (chrome:// pages, the Web Store, the PDF viewer). Try it on your app's tab.
The shortcut does nothingHold the mouse over Pointer's icon: it names the key Chrome gave Pointer, or says none is set. Another app may use the key too. Open chrome://extensions/shortcuts and give Pointer a key of your own, or click the icon.
No file and lineYour app is not in development mode, or its framework has no source information. Your agent still gets each element's text and selector, which is enough to find it.
A file:// pageOpen chrome://extensions, then Pointer's Details, and turn on Allow access to file URLs.

Tools and keys

On a phone-sized page (Chrome DevTools' device mode) the bar keeps Pick, Comment, Draw, Undo and Copy; ⋯ More opens the rest. Touch works: tap to comment or pick, drag to draw or move.

C CommentClick anywhere, type, Enter. Click a number to edit it, drag it to move it.
P PickEdit an element's CSS live: click it and its style box opens. ↑ ↓ walk to its parent or child, Enter takes it. Your changes go into the copy.
Q IdeaClick what could be better and add your thought if you like. Your agent answers as a designer would: what is wrong and why, 2–3 fixes, and it asks before building.
X CropClick a component or drag a frame. Several crops make one picture.
G GapDrag from one element to another: Pointer measures the space between them. Its note: smaller, bigger or the gap you want.
D A B E TPen, arrow, box, ellipse, text. Shift gives straight lines, squares and circles. On the bar they share one button with Gap: it shows the last one you used, and ▾ opens the others. Double-click a box or an ellipse to type on it.
M MoveDrag marks and pasted images. Delete removes the one under the mouse.
L IconClick the spot where an icon should go: a small picker opens there with five quick icons, and More searches all of Lucide's. Pick one and it lands on that spot. Drag it to move it; its corner resizes it.
⌘V Ctrl+VPaste a screenshot or a mockup onto the page. [ ] make it see-through.
⌘C Ctrl+CCopy. The ▾ next to Copy chooses picture + text, text only or picture only, shows what each costs, and opens a preview.
⌘Z Ctrl+ZUndo the last mark, or the last Clear everything.
⌘⇧Z Ctrl+Shift+Z Ctrl+YRedo: puts back what Undo took.
⇧⌫ Shift+DeleteClear everything. Changed your mind? Click Undo in the message, or press ⌘Z Ctrl+Z.

Privacy

Pointer runs only on the tab where you turn it on, and only until you turn it off. It sends nothing: what you mark goes to your clipboard, and you decide where to paste it. So that a reload does not wipe your marks, Chrome keeps them in memory for that tab and page until the tab or Chrome closes, you clear everything or you close Pointer. Nothing is written to disk or synced. The privacy policy.
